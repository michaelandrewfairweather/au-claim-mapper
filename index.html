What are my options, and how long do I have?

Answer a few plain questions to see which routes may be open to you, the usual time limits, the costs risks, and when to talk to a lawyer.

This is general legal information, not legal advice. It can't take account of everything about your situation. Time limits can sometimes be extended, and other rules may apply. Before you rely on any date or choose a route, talk to a lawyer, union or community legal centre. Nothing you enter leaves this page.

If you or someone else is in danger, call 000. If you're in distress, Lifeline is on 13 11 14. If you need an interpreter, TIS National is on 131 450.

      When to talk to a lawyer

      Talk to a lawyer before you rely on any date here, before you choose between routes, and straight away if a time limit is less than six months away or may have passed. This tool can't weigh the details of your situation; a lawyer can.

      • To find a lawyer, the law society or law institute in your state or territory runs a referral service.
      • For free help, try Legal Aid in your state or territory, or a community legal centre. For work problems, your union if you're a member.
      • Ask about costs at the start. A lawyer should explain how they will charge you before they begin.
      • Take with you the dates you entered here, any letters or emails about the problem, and the names of anyone involved.
