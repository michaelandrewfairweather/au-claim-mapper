Do I still have time to make a claim?

If you've been hurt in Victoria, answer a few plain questions to see the usual time limit for a personal injury claim, and when to talk to a lawyer.

This is general legal information, not legal advice. It can't take account of everything about your situation. Time limits can sometimes be extended, and other rules may apply. Before you rely on any date, talk to a lawyer or a community legal centre. Nothing you enter leaves this page.

If you or someone else is in danger, call 000. If you're in distress, Lifeline is on 13 11 14. If you need an interpreter, TIS National is on 131 450.

    Law used

      When to talk to a lawyer

      Talk to a lawyer before you rely on any date here, and straight away if a time limit is less than six months away or may have passed. This tool can't weigh the details of your situation; a lawyer can.

      • To find a lawyer, the Law Institute of Victoria runs a referral service. In other states and territories, the law society does.
      • For free help, try Victoria Legal Aid or a community legal centre. If you were hurt at work, your union can help if you're a member.
      • Ask about costs at the start. A lawyer should explain how they will charge you before they begin.
      • Take with you the dates you entered here, any letters or emails about the problem, and the names of anyone involved.
